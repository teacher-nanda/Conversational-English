## 1. Her core lesson philosophy
- CELTA-trained (Boston, 2019). Every lesson follows a CELTA stage logic: Lead-in > Exposure (text/audio) > Analysis of examples > Clarification (Meaning, Pronunciation, Form, with CCQs) > Controlled practice > Freer practice > Feedback (content + language).
- Test-Teach-Test for vocabulary and functional language: a diagnostic task first, then teach only what students miss.
- Speaking lessons: lead-in, demo of the task, language for the task, planning time, task, feedback on content, feedback on language.
- Plans use: Aims (SWBAT), Assumptions, Anticipated problems + solutions, Personal aims + strategies, Interaction patterns, Board plan.
- Beginner rule: always write the instructions, do many demos, speak slowly, use gestures and subtitles.
- Lessons should end with real-life output: students produce something (a recording, a dialogue, a message).
- Always connect text/audio to the teaching point; students need an authentic reason to use the language.

## 2. Course structure she designed (Course Framework)
1. Lesson: full CELTA structure on a unit or part of a unit.
2. Sum-up / worksheet: PDF theory recap plus optional extra exercises (Grammar Bank, Vocabulary Bank, Useful Words, anything unused).
3. Daily Challenge: (a) pronunciation, (b) review of earlier content not yet used, (c) authentic material to watch/listen/read and respond to.
4. Speaking lesson: original lesson based on previous content, in groups or individual for shy students.
5. Weekly bonus game and bonus tasks for extra points.
6. Learner autonomy kit: Learning Journal (goal, new language + situations, 1 thing I liked, 1 thing to improve, next lesson I will use...), practice plan (homework 2 days before class; review at 1, 3, 5, 10, 15 days), notebook tools (Google Site, Padlet, Quizlet, Slides).

## 3. Her bank of activity types (Types of Activities)
- Fill the gaps: sentences, dialogues, with a word bank, with a hint in brackets (verb + tense).
- Put sentences in order; create sentences from given words + a tense.
- Write or say short/long forms (I'm = I am).
- Listen and repeat: vocabulary, functional language, grammar, stress, same-sound words (IPA).
- Sort words into columns by sound (house/hi vs rat/raspberry).
- Listen and match, order a dialogue, match image to meaning, match music to country.
- Listen and circle which of two you hear; listen and fill gaps.
- Guess something from an image (nationality), then check.
- Watch a video and answer: gaps, multiple choice, synonyms.
- Correct wrong sentences (with or without the error in bold).
- True/False and then correct the false ones.

## 4. Games and creative activities she has already imagined (reuse and adapt)
- Secret code in a bottle: number-to-letter code, students decode words (Thursday, Meet, Sorry...) then write sentences with them.
- Lock/escape-room idea: every correct answer gives a color or piece; students enter the colors in a form (Qualtrics/Forms) to open the lock.
- Guess which number I am: pairs with number cards (Are you number 5? No, I'm not).
- Battleship in pairs to practice letters and numbers (Hit & Miss).
- Bingo with spelled-out numbers; dictation of numbers.
- Zoom-out image game: guess the object while the picture zooms out, answer in a full sentence (What are they? They are keys).
- Memory game: image disappears after 1 minute, students list and then write sentences.
- Jamboard-style matching: drag country names and flags onto images.
- Collaborative sound game: teams get texts and a "mega word"; they find words with the same sound; the team pools words to unlock a challenge.
- Pronunciation game: -ed ending, 5 rounds of 4 verbs, choose /t/, /d/ or /id/, then show the correction.
- Public Kahoot / international Kahoot review after each unit block.
- Comic strip dialogue: pick images or draw, write a dialogue (souvenir shop).
- Number rating task: rate 1-10 how important things are (pizza, family, Instagram, English) and share in written form.
- Tongue twisters for TH sounds.
- Family tree from a video, then 5 sentences.
- Quizzes on Stories, saved by category in highlights (Instagram idea).

## 5. Learn with Nanda 2025: clubs and check-ins
Check-in lessons (25 min): review goals with the SMART framework, reflect on the learning path, or free talk; offer other lesson types or a club.
Club frameworks:
- Book Club: story read in advance; discussion questions; roles (Storyteller, Character Analyzer, Word Hunter, Artist); teacher gives feedback and a vocabulary review.
- Travel Club: students research places/sights/cuisine in advance if they want; teacher prepares everything; compare cultures and countries.
- Gaming Club: one game every 2 lessons (Codenames, Dixit, Taboo, Fishbowl, Pictionary...), chosen by students; focus on vocabulary, conversation, fluency, writing.
- Conversational Club: material sent in advance; daily-life discussions plus small projects; teacher gives feedback.

Travel Club episodes (topic > grammar focus > vocabulary), a ready-made lesson idea bank:
1. Previous travel experiences > past simple + past continuous (While we were walking... I was eating when...) > itinerary, adventure, wanderlust
2. Planning a trip yourself > advice, recommendations, imperatives > budget, accommodation, reservation
3. Most beautiful places > descriptive adjectives/adverbs > scenic, picturesque, breathtaking
4. Most disappointing places > comparatives and superlatives > tourist trap, overrated, underwhelming
5. Delicious cuisine > food and taste description > savory, flavorful, tangy
6. Cool activities abroad > instructions with sequencers (First, Then) > souvenir, authentic, immersion
7. Beliefs and superstitions > conditionals 1 and 2, "it is considered..." > taboo, folklore
8. Festivals and traditions > describing events > ritual, ceremony, vibrant
9. 7 Wonders of the Modern World > present perfect (Have you ever...?) > landmark, iconic
10. Best photo spots > passive voice > panorama, perspective
11. Museums > opinions and reasons > exhibit, curator, thought-provoking
12. Fun hotel experiences > narrating past events > amenities, hospitality
13. High mountains and tall buildings > comparisons > summit, altitude, skyline
14. Souvenirs and memories
Idea: in every episode give students question cards they can choose from, to keep conversation flowing.

## 6. Conversation Group (2020 project, with Karina)
- 1 h, 2 to 6 students, two teachers.
- Structure: topic introduction by students, discussion (everyone asks questions and gives opinions), wrap-up feedback on expressions, grammar, vocabulary, pronunciation and tips.
- After class: worksheet by email + feedback questionnaire to suggest topics.
- Students are grouped by similar age range; first lesson free.

## 8. Course types she offers (Trial Lesson structure)
Conversational English; Complete English (4 skills); English with series/movies/music (student picks the episode, writes down expressions, summarizes, gives opinion, situations); TOEFL/TOEIC/IELTS prep; Business English (meetings, interviews); Customized.
Standard conversational lesson flow: student presents hands-on project > homework feedback > game on the topic > warm-up > language review of expressions from a video > input (watch/listen/read + questions) > opinion speaking + language feedback > "In what situations can you use these?" > Quizlet list (terms, meanings, situations) > homework.
Skills map for diagnosis: Grammar (past tenses, overusing will, articles, comparatives, relative clauses, passive, conditionals); Vocabulary (-ed/-ing adjectives, phrasal verbs, expressions, idioms, chunks, functional language); Fluency (thinking time, connectors, statements); Pronunciation (sounds, syllable stress, sentence stress, intonation).
WWW / EBI self-review after each lesson (What Went Well / Even Better If).

## 10. How to use this when creating a lesson
1. Start from her CELTA stage logic and add a clickable/game element from section 4.
2. Pick a speaking format that differs from the last lesson (see AUDIT-CHECKLIST rule 46).
3. For a travel, culture or hobby topic, borrow the Travel Club grammar+vocabulary pairings.
4. Offer a Daily Challenge-style homework: pronunciation + review + authentic material.
5. Add a hook from the Instagram list (US vs UK, false cognates, idiom of the day) when the target language allows.
6. Mention a learning-journal or WWW/EBI closing line when suitable.

## 11. CELTA teaching-practice games (proven formats, 2019)
- Mystery clue hunt (Speaking TP 5): demo video of the task, language for negotiation (I think this is mine because...), clues hidden around, each team may hold only 5 sentences, groups negotiate swaps; feedback on content (how did you convince them?) and on language. Language point: present continuous for future plans. Superhero theme; works as detective/clue scenarios.
- Snowman (hangman) lead-in with famous lines from a show, to reveal the lesson topic (Friends: "We were on a break").
- Friends clip as grammar context (simple past): gist questions, words from the episode put in order to build 2 affirmative + 1 negative + 1 question sentences, guided-discovery rules, Pictionary/mime to elicit verbs and their past forms, then a "special moment in your life" story as freer practice.
- Listening lesson (Evolve 4 TP 1): predict from the picture, check predictions, inference question ("Will she get the job? Why?"), then find a classmate with similar answers, then mock job interview as extension.
- Other game materials she built: STOP! game, Heads Up cards, Find-the-word game, Pictionary verb list, "Events and reasons" sheet, superhero characteristics sheet, Comparative/Superlative survey, Simple Sound Maze (pronunciation).
- CELTA personal aims worth keeping: reduce teacher talking time, vary interaction patterns and partners, efficient guided discovery (only the essentials, with answer key so students self-correct), always plan anticipated problems and solutions, and ICQs for every task.
- Stage terms she uses: Lead-in, Exposure, Analysis of examples, Clarification (MPF), Controlled practice, Freer practice, Extension task (always have one ready in case time is left).

## 12. Teaching Teens Online (webinar notes, J. Arriola)
- Start with a needs analysis (age, interests, tools they know, skills, why English); deliver by email form or a getting-to-know-you activity; plan from the results.
- Warm-ups: tongue twisters, riddles (What goes up and never comes down? Your age), web quests, online scavenger hunt, ABC game, Half the picture, Tutti Frutti, What's missing, lip-reading, soundtracks (Guess the film), realia (What's in the box?).
- Grammar/vocabulary systems: jumble sentences, draughts and noughts-and-crosses with verb squares, Yes/No guessing game, Scrabble, football-field game, What's missing.
- Reading: jigsaw reading, reading race (questions posted at stations), students write 5 questions and swap groups, post-reading retell with pictures, role-play interview, continue or change the story.
- Listening: keyword prediction (10-12 words, 2-3 decoys), dictogloss (note 10-12 words, rebuild the text, then see the script), intensive listening (transcribe a video they like, compare with script).
- Speaking and writing: Chalk and cheese discussion game, retell a silent video, write a prequel/sequel, emoji stories (describe your weekend with emojis, partner guesses), random-category story cards (person, place, feeling, transport...).
- Treat teens as adults, use authentic material, keep variety.

## 13. Assessment ideas (Building Learner-Oriented Assessments, TEFL Horizons)
- Diagnostic first (speaking prompt cards, ice-breaker, writing + speaking), then formative checks, then summative.
- Formative formats: low-stakes quizzes, group discussion (students write questions, interview other groups, report back), group projects, roleplays (homestay), graphic organizers, think-pair-share, learner-generated quizzes, self-assessment (I want to be able to...), exit ticket (one thing I learned, one question I have), peer assessment with guidelines, Start-Stop-Continue for the teacher.
- Share the rubric before the task, show a strong and a weak sample, allow drafts and resubmission, give a self-check list, give forward-looking feedback ("Next time you could...", pick 1-2 things to work on this week), and include reflection.

## 14. Activity techniques from her methodology books (PELT, activities notes)

## 16. Digital storytelling (Coursera course notes)
